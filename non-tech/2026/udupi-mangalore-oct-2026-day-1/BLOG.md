## How did it come to be?

Soumyadeep is the brain behind this trip. He and I have been friends since our JEE coaching days (junior college/11th-12th standard). We met each other randomly in a sharing rickshaw one day and travelled back to Railway Station together everyday since then.

My home was near to the railway station, but he travelled from a different city altogether! This is the reality of entrance exams in this country, kids are made to travel vast distances every single day for coaching in hopes of making it big someday.

We recognized this was an hassle but never spent time complaining about it. Somehow him and me always used to talk about the most random ass shit on the ride back. This continued for two years, which at times is just a phase for life, whose friendships you let slip past with the phase. But few people stay, Soumyadeep has been one of them. We literally have nothing in common, our degrees, professions, interests, residence, none. Thankfully, that hasn't stopped us from staying in touch!

And hence, when we said he would be coming to Bengaluru for a conference, it was already decided he was hanging out with me for the rest of the stay. He then pitched the trip and that's how it came to be. We decided the long weekend of 2nd to 4th October would be the perfect time. Another local friend of his named Rakesh decided to join us. And and so the planning began! Rakesh wanted to do the Ettina Bhuja hike and Soumyadeep wanted to enjoy Udupi and Mangalore. With places decided, Soumyadeep took on the responsibility of travel and booked the onwards and return trains. Rakes handled the trek tickets. And I was incharge of hotel bookings...which I conveniently forgot about. 🙈 

## Brave on Dose

We had an early morning `05:45` train from Yeswanthpur station to Sakleshpur. We had pre-booked an Uber and hence reached there on time. We had some time to spare and hence decided to prepare our sleep-deprived bodies for the upcoming long day--with coffee!

We headed to a Darshini nearby and ordered Masala Doses with some hot filter coffee. By the time we received two of those, Rakesh had arrived too! This was my first conversation with him. Having known enough people named Rakesh, all of whom have been A-rated evil people, I had a bad mental image. Thankfully, this Rakesh broke all the bad assumptions. In hindsight I should have know this, considering he is Soumyadeep's friend.

By the time we completed some short talk over food, we realized it was 20 minutes away for train's departure. But then I noticed Soumyadeep hadn't had his Dose. We were never delivered the third Dose!! As it was a self serve system, we looked over the counter and realized it had gotten so crowded that they had lost the track of our Dose. Soumyadeep grew reluctant to get it cause of the train, but I also knew he was super hungry from our earlier conversations in the cab!

I am usually not an outgoing person, but certain circumstances open the pathway to let my demons out. "WE HAVE PAID FOR IT" is what I shouted and rushed directly to the counter. Sadly, the crowd wasn't forgiving and I had to fight the owner to get the food we paid for half an hour prior, delivered to us! Thankfully, it arrived in time and we were able to catch the train.

A heated start to the trip, definitely not a bad omen.

We caught up with our sleep in the train and reached Sakleshpur by `11:00` AM. Now `Ettina Bhuja` is 30-40 km away from the main city and hence we needed an auto reach there. We tried haggling with auto drivers on the station itself, but they were too rude to even talk to. I have noticed this pattern when they deal with non-Kannanda speakers, but Rakesh was a localite and still we faced this problem. We decided to walk to the bus station and try catching an auto from there.

It was a nice walk around the lake and luckily we found one willing to take us at reasonable prices. I covered exact hike experience in this blog [here]((/blog/ettina-bhuja/).

After the exhausting hike, I was already feeling super groggy. And hence we decided to stop for Coffee on our way back. It was a random cafe in the middle of no-where. I wasn't expecting much, but even that wasn't enough. Their coffee was amongst the worst coffee I have ever had! Absolute lowest in the charts. Something I find puzzling: there's filter coffee everywhere in and around Bengaluru. What is up with Sakleshpur, there were no coffee points ANYWHERE! And the single one we tried was SO BAD. I am pretty sure a lot of coffee grows in that area cause I could see loads of plantation while enroute to the hike. Sadly, locals don't seem interested in sipping some of their harvest.

We hadn't had any breakfast in the morning and after the worst coffee, we needed to have some good food. We asked for the best local Darshini around, of which there was just one open that day. We gave a large order and it was slightly off time, start of the evening, and hence they were taking their own sweet time to prepare our order. I was determined to get the taste of that coffee off my mouth immediately, and hence I ended up in a bakery nearby. Luckily, a `Cream Bun` food critic found it's perfect target. I ordered the cream bun and I would rate it `2.5/5`. `0.5` brownie points for it finding me at the point I needed it the most!
