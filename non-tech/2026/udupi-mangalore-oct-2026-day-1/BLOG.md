I recently (2nd-4th October 2026) went on a three day trip to Sakleshpur, Udupi and Mangalore. This post is a first installment in a three part blog series detailing my adventures.

## How did it come to be?

Soumyadeep is the brain behind this trip. He and I have been friends since our JEE coaching days (junior college/11th-12th standard). We met each other randomly in a sharing rickshaw one day. We realized we were in the same class and heading in the same direction. From that point onwards, we travelled back to the railway station together after classes.

My home was near the railway station, but he travelled from a different city altogether! This is the reality of entrance exams in this country, kids are made to travel vast distances every single day in hopes of making it big someday. He recognized this was a hassle but never complained about it.

<p align="center">
<img src="https://images.fknil.com/blogs/udupi-mangalore-oct-2026-day-1/mushroom-on-dead-tree-1.webp" alt="Mushroom on dead tree" />
</p>

We used to talk about the most random ass shit on the ride back. This continued for two years. Small enough time to be a passing by phase in life. Thankfully, our friendship wasn't. Few people stay. Soumyadeep has been one of them. We literally have nothing in common, our degrees, professions, interests, residence, none. Though, none of that has stopped us from staying in touch!

And hence, when he told me he was coming to Bengaluru for a conference, I already knew how he was spending the rest of his stay. He then pitched the trip and that's how it came to be. We decided the long weekend of 2nd to 4th October would be the perfect time. Another local friend of his named Rakesh decided to join us. And so the planning began! Rakesh wanted to do the Ettina Bhuja hike and Soumyadeep wanted to enjoy Udupi and Mangalore. With places decided, Soumyadeep took on the responsibility of travel. He booked the outward and return trains. Rakesh handled the trek tickets. And I was in charge of hotel bookings...which I conveniently forgot about. 🙈

## Brave on dose

Our train to Sakleshpur was supposed to arrive at Yewanthpur by `05:45`. It was early in the morning and we didn't want to risk it, so we pre-booked an Uber the night before. We reached early enough and had some time to spare. Both of us were yawning uncontrollably and hence decided to prepare our sleep-deprived bodies for the upcoming long day with a coffee!

We headed to a darshini nearby and ordered masala doses with some hot filter coffee. By the time our first two doses arrived, Rakesh was there too! This was my first time meeting him in person. Walking into the conversation, I had a bad mental image. I have known quite a few people named Rakesh, and all of them have been A-rated evil people. Thankfully, this Rakesh broke all the bad assumptions. In hindsight, I should have known this, considering he is Soumyadeep's good friend.

<p align="center">
<img src="https://images.fknil.com/blogs/udupi-mangalore-oct-2026-day-1/mushroom-on-dead-tree-2.webp" alt="Mushroom on dead tree" />
</p>

By the time we completed some small talk over food, we realized there were 20 minutes left for the train's departure. But then I noticed Soumyadeep hadn't had his dose. We were never delivered the third dose!! As it was a self-service system, we looked over the counter, and realized it had gotten so crowded that they had lost the track of our third dose. Soumyadeep grew reluctant to get it cause of the train, but I also knew he was super hungry from our earlier conversations in the cab.

I am usually not an outgoing person, but certain circumstances open the pathway to let my demons out. "WE HAVE PAID FOR IT, WE WILL GET IT". With that slogan, I rushed to the counter. Sadly, the crowd wasn't forgiving. I had to argue with the owner to get the food we had paid for half an hour prior! Thankfully, it arrived in time and we were able to catch the train.

A heated start to the trip, definitely not a bad omen.

We caught up on sleep on the train and reached Sakleshpur by `11:00`. `Ettina Bhuja` is around 30-40 km away from the main city and hence we needed a rickshaw to reach there. We tried haggling with rickshaw drivers outside the railway station, but they were too rude for us to continue. This is a pretty common occurrence with non-Kannada speakers, but in this case we had pushed Rakesh ahead to talk. And he's a local guy! After the unsuccessful attempt at getting a ride, we decided to walk to the bus station and try finding a ride from there.

Sakleshpur's bus station is around 1.2 km away from the railway station. Displacement between both is super small, but the distance is long because of a lake. It was a nice walk around the lake and luckily we found a rickshaw driver willing to take us at reasonable prices. I covered the rest of the hike experience in this blog [here](/blog/ettina-bhuja/).

After an exhausting hike, I was already feeling super groggy. Hence we decided to stop for a coffee on our way back. It was a random cafe in the middle of nowhere. I wasn't expecting much, but even that wasn't enough. Their coffee was amongst the worst coffee I have ever had! Absolute lowest in the charts. Something I find puzzling: there's filter coffee everywhere in and around Bengaluru. What is up with Sakleshpur? There was no good coffee to be found! I am pretty sure a lot of coffee grows in that area cause I could see plantations en route to the hike. Sadly, locals don't seem interested in sipping some of their harvest.

It didn't help that our last meal was `05:00` in the morning. At this point, we were in dire need of some good food. We asked for the best local darshini around, of which there was just one open on that day. For what seemed like a never ending order, we got a nice smile from the waiter for giving them some business `:)`. It was around `16:00`, well pass lunchtime. The kitchen didn't have a lot of ingredients prepared and ready to be used. As they were taking their own sweet time to prepare our order, I was determined to get the taste of that coffee out my mouth immediately. Fortunately, just before entering the darshini, I had noticed a bakery nearby. And with sheer luck, a `Cream Bun` food critic found its perfect target. Learning from my previous mistakes I kept my expectations absolutely low. Good thing I did. I would rate it `2.5/5`. `0.5` brownie points for it finding me at the point I needed it the most!

Our next order of business was figuring out where to go. As we hadn't planned anything, we could go to Udupi or Mangalore either. Worst part, we kept swinging between both options. None of us was able to decid what to do 😭. Best potential tie breaker was going to be us finding an accommodation in [Shri Krishna Matha](https://maps.app.goo.gl/NDnVxeUT85R3dvyGA) in Udupi. Sadly, even that fell through. In the end, we played choose-a-finger game and boarded a bus to Mangalore! Thought, we were yet to figure out an accommodation.

## Let us in

We had just boarded the bus and had some spare energy from the recent gluttonous attack on food. This energy was beamed at finding hotels in Mangalore. We opened maps, searched for all the hotels available and started calling them one after the other. "No room saar" beep beep. After hearing that around 20-30 times, it started to dawn upon us: we might be in a hairy situation regarding accommodation. It was not wise to underestimate the long weekend. We finally decided to give up on calling and planned on asking rickshaw drivers to take us to different hotels they have tie up with. Maybe all we need is an inside agent, who takes some commission, but gets the job done. After so many conversations, my social battery was dead. Just in time, insulin spike gently carried me into a nice nap.

We reached Mangalore around `21:00`. Time to pour the regained energy into accommodation hunt! We started knocking on doors of all the hotels near the bus stand. There was some hope in doing this cause a bunch of them weren't listed on Google Maps. To our dismay, none had any rooms left. Soumyadeep was checking hotel listings on `MakeMyTrip` and one of the owners was responding very positively. In desperation, we decided to go to the rough location provided by the in-app map. A bunch of lost boys, walking around the locality, trying to hang onto this sliver of hope. It appeared we were getting close to the guest house and owner was responding nicely, almost offering us a booking. No jinx, please? please? When we finally got owner's contact and were about to tell him that we are ready to check in, he cancelled the booking 😭.

Okay, now our only hopes are rickshaw drivers. Ma Messiah. We found a guy and asked him to show us hotels nearby. He drove us around ~2km away, towards the center of the city. We stopped at a decent looking hotel in the middle of a one way road. This time we had more than a sliver, this was an industrial thread. It was woven by driver's boasts about his personal relations here. He had called and asked about empty rooms before accepting the ride. Guess what? It was FULL too! Fuck this shit. At this point, Rakesh was half panicking. We started walking alongside the road, and I spotted a rusty board advertising a hotel. This ain't no mirage. The rundown building we were looking at had few of its floors converted into a hotel. To reach reception, we had to enter a super shady looking basement. I started preparing to make a deal with the devil.

<p align="center">
<img src="https://images.fknil.com/blogs/udupi-mangalore-oct-2026-day-1/let-me-in.webp" alt="LET ME IN" />
</p>

Guess what? This was FULL too! Or so the receptionist said. This is where it gets interesting and something I have learnt about from this experience. Mid-tier hotels will always say there are no empty rooms during rush hours. But if you insist enough and walk with one of the employees to see a room at higher rates/higher grade, he might sneakily offer you a deal. In our case, receptionist said he only had an expensive "suite" available. We walked with the doorman to see the room. Once we were inside, he offered us an AC room on the same floor! Ma Messiah!

He asked us to quote a price and I didn't want to lose this opportunity. Naturally, I quoted a slightly higher rate. In hindsight, I could have haggled and received the room for a much lower price. Lessons learnt.

And hence, we finally found a temporary place to call home sweet home for the next two days.

## Shake dat

We all freshened up and were ready to put the tummy growls at rest. Rakesh was somehow hungry enough that he wanted to have a complete biryani! I cannot finish a full serving of biryani even on the best of my days. Soumyadeep and I weren't as hungry. We decided to have a small shake and a few fries at a nearby cafe called [Shake Factory](https://maps.app.goo.gl/uhDhcsnFLB8S2hV28). We ordered `Nutty chocolate` and `Cream & Cookies` shake alongside some loaded french fries.

Shakes were fantabulous. And the fries, oh boy they were loaded. Not just with toppings, but in quantity too 😭. Soumyadeep and I together barely finished the combo. We were so full, we had to take a 3-4 km walk around the city to feel better. At least this place isn't an active participant of shrinkflation yet `:)`.

Loads of movement plus awesome food. Perfect recipe for a good night's sleep.
